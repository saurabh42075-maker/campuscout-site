Going abroad is a money decision wearing an education costume

The course matters. So does the country, the loan, the work rights afterwards and what the degree is actually worth when your child comes home. We work through all of it, including the parts that argue against going.

The question nobody asks

Some universities charge you ₹40 lakh. Some pay you to attend.

Most study-abroad consultants are paid a commission by the universities they send students to. That is not a scandal — it is just how the industry works. But it has a consequence almost nobody says out loud: the universities that pay commission are the ones charging full fees. The funded routes pay agents nothing, so nobody shows them to you.

We look at both. For a strong student in the right field, a funded place can turn a forty-lakh decision into a four-lakh one.

Assistantships in the US and Canada

A graduate assistantship is a paid teaching or research role attached to your admission. At most universities it waives tuition entirely, pays a monthly stipend, and subsidises health insurance. International students are eligible. Appointments typically run 10–20 hours a week, and in the US, federal rules cap international students at 20 hours during term.

Tuition-free public universities in Europe

Most public universities in Germany charge no tuition at all to international students — only a semester contribution, which DAAD puts at €70–430. There are real exceptions: Baden-Württemberg charges non-EU students €1,500 per semester, and TUM in Bavaria charges €4,000–6,000 per semester for master's programmes. The difference between picking the right state and the wrong one is several lakh rupees a year.

Being straight with you

This is not available to everyone, and we will tell you if it is not

Funded places are competitive and concentrated — research degrees more than taught ones, and some fields far more than others. Your marks, your research background and the strength of your written application all decide it. A student who is not a realistic candidate should hear that from us early, while there is still time to plan something sensible, rather than after four months of hope and application fees.

How this is paid for

Our India work is free. This part is not, and here is why.

School counselling and Indian college admissions cost you nothing, because colleges pay us a referral fee when a student enrols. We have said that on every page since the day we started.

Funded study abroad does not work that way. The universities that waive your tuition and pay you a stipend do not pay agents. So there is no commission to collect, and anyone offering to guide you to them "for free" is either not doing the work or is quietly steering you somewhere that does pay them.

So we charge for it directly, and we publish what we know for free. Read the guides, do it yourself, and never speak to us — that is a completely legitimate outcome and the guides are written to make it possible.

Start here

Four questions before you look at a single university

01

What is the total number?

Not tuition. Tuition plus living costs plus travel plus visa plus insurance, times the length of the course, at a realistic exchange rate. Write it down before you fall in love with a campus.

02

How is it being paid?

Savings, education loan, or both. If it is a loan, the repayment starts whether or not the job does. We will run that maths with you rather than around you.

03

What happens after the degree?

Post-study work rights differ sharply by country and change with politics. A two-year work window and a six-month one are completely different investments.

04

Would the same money go further in India?

Sometimes yes. We will say so. A good Indian programme plus the saved capital beats a weak foreign one more often than anyone in this industry admits.

The runway

Twelve to eighteen months, worked backwards

Almost every abroad application that goes wrong went wrong on timing, not on merit.

1

18–12 months out

Country and course shortlist. Budget agreed with the family. IELTS or TOEFL booked with room to retake.

2

12–6 months out

Applications, SOP drafted properly rather than downloaded, LORs requested early, scholarship deadlines mapped.

3

6–0 months out

Offers, loan sanction, proof of funds, visa file, accommodation. This stage is paperwork discipline, nothing else.

Questions parents ask

Straight answers

Is an agent the same as a counsellor?

No. An agent is paid to place students with particular universities. A counsellor should be working out what is right for your child first. Where an institution pays us, we say so openly and will name it if you ask. Funded routes pay nobody, so for those we charge you directly and agree the fee first.

My child's marks are average. Is abroad still possible?

Often yes — many good foreign universities weigh the application as a whole rather than a single percentage. Whether it is worth the money is the harder question, and that is the one we will actually discuss with you.

How early should we start?

Class 11 is not too early for the money conversation. For applications, twelve to eighteen months before the intake. Families who start six months out end up choosing from what is left.

What about education loans?

We will help you understand collateral requirements, moratorium periods and what banks look for, and refer you to lenders. We do not take commission from any lender.
